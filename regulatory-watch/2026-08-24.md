## Cayman Islands

### ⚠️ URGENT — CIMA new AML Rule and Sanctions Rule — mandatory compliance deadline September 18, 2026
**What changed:** On July 20, 2026, CIMA published a new Rule on Effective Compliance Programme for the Prevention and Detection of Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Proliferation Financing and a new Rule on Compliance with Financial Sanctions and Targeted Financial Sanctions. Both rules apply to all CIMA registered or licensed financial service providers including all registered investment funds. The rules enter into force September 18, 2026.
**What it means for clients:** All Cayman investment funds and their CIMA-licensed service providers must have updated AML compliance programmes and sanctions screening procedures in place by September 18, 2026; clients with Cayman fund structures should immediately confirm readiness with their Cayman fund administrators and directors.
**Source:** Walkers Global — 2026-07-20 — https://www.walkersglobal.com/en/Insights/2026/07/Cayman-Islands-new-AML-Rule-and-new-Sanctions-Rule

### 👁 WATCH — Cayman Islands DITC closing CRS XML portal for transition to CRS XML Schema v3.0
**What changed:** The Cayman Islands DITC Portal closed for submission of CRS XMLs and Filing Declarations in early August 2026 to accommodate the transition to CRS XML Schema v3.0 (CRS 2.0, in force from January 1, 2026). The CRS 2.0 regime brings digital assets — including e-money wallets, CBDCs, crypto derivatives, and stablecoins — into CRS reporting scope. The DITC will no longer provide an XML generator tool.
**What it means for clients:** Cayman-based fund administrators and financial institutions must source their own CRS XML generation tools for the v3.0 format; additionally, clients with Cayman funds that hold or administer digital assets will for the first time have those assets reported under CRS, expanding the information exchange footprint.
**Source:** TAINA Technology — 2026-08-01 — https://www.taina.tech/resources/common-reporting-standard-amendments-2026

### ℹ️ FYI — Cayman Islands tokenised fund legislation in force since March 24, 2026
**What changed:** The Mutual Funds (Amendment) Act, 2026 and Private Funds (Amendment) Act, 2026 came into force on March 24, 2026, adding statutory definitions and operating requirements for tokenised mutual and private funds. Tokenised funds may issue digital tokens representing fund interests without triggering the VASP regime; they remain regulated under existing funds legislation with additional obligations. CIMA has supervisory powers over tokenised fund technology and digital token transactions.
**What it means for clients:** Cayman fund structures may now issue digital tokens representing fund interests under the existing regulatory framework without triggering VASP registration, providing a new structuring option for clients interested in offering tokenised private fund interests while remaining in the established CIMA-regulated environment.
**Source:** Maples Group — 2026-03-24 — https://maples.com/knowledge/cayman-islands-tokenised-fund-legislation-2026

## Panama

### 👁 WATCH — Panama Law 526 of 2026 introduces economic substance requirements for foreign passive income — applies from 2027 fiscal year
**What changed:** Enacted May 28, 2026, Panama Law 526 requires entities in multinational groups receiving foreign-source passive income (dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains, real estate income) to demonstrate genuine economic substance in Panama to maintain zero-tax treatment. Entities failing the substance test pay 15% on net taxable income. Panama retains its territorial system but now requires actual rather than merely formal presence, effective from the 2027 fiscal year.
**What it means for clients:** Clients using Panama holding companies, foundations or SPVs to receive foreign-source passive income must demonstrate genuine economic substance in Panama from 2027 or face a 15% tax on net passive income, potentially eliminating the primary tax benefit of Panama structures.
**Source:** KPMG — 2026-05-28 — https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/panama-economic-substance-passive-income.html

## USA (Federal)

### ℹ️ FYI — FinCEN permanently eliminates BOI reporting for US companies and US persons — effective August 14, 2026
**What changed:** Effective August 14, 2026, FinCEN issued a final rule permanently exempting US person beneficial owners from BOI reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act. Foreign entities that are reporting companies must still report BOI for foreign individual beneficial owners. FinCEN also announced it will delete previously reported information by US persons from the BOI database.
**What it means for clients:** US persons and US-domiciled entities are permanently exempt from CTA beneficial ownership reporting, eliminating the compliance burden and associated costs; however, foreign entities with non-US beneficial owners that qualify as reporting companies remain subject to BOI disclosure requirements.

## USA (Florida)

### ℹ️ FYI — Florida SB 264 foreign buyer real estate restrictions remain in force after November 2025 legal uphold
**What changed:** Florida's Senate Bill 264 (effective July 1, 2023), restricting nationals from seven 'countries of concern' (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela, Syria) from purchasing real estate, was upheld in November 2025 and continues to be enforceable in 2026. Chinese nationals face a near-total ban; nationals from other six countries face restrictions within 10 miles of military installations or critical infrastructure. Buyers must sign affidavits; violations carry potential property forfeiture and criminal charges.
**What it means for clients:** Clients who are nationals of China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela, or Syria face binding legal restrictions on purchasing Florida real estate; Chinese national clients face a near-total ban and must be advised before any Florida property transaction to avoid forfeiture risk and criminal exposure.

## Brazil

### ℹ️ FYI — BCB/CVM Joint Resolution 13/2024 — non-resident investors must use same instruments as residents, in force since January 1, 2025
**What changed:** In force since January 1, 2025, Article 5 of Joint Resolution BCB/CVM 13/2024 requires non-resident investment to be conducted in the same financial instruments and modalities available to resident investors, with equivalent registration requirements and operational limits. This standardises market access for foreign capital and eliminates prior carve-outs for non-resident investment vehicles.
**What it means for clients:** Non-resident clients investing in Brazil can no longer rely on previous carve-outs for foreign investment vehicles and must use standard Brazilian investment instruments with the same registration, reporting and operational limits applicable to residents, increasing administrative requirements for Brazil investment portfolios.

## BVI

### ℹ️ FYI — BVI beneficial ownership register open to 'legitimate interest' access since April 1, 2026
**What changed:** Following the January 1, 2026 filing deadline (with a moratorium on penalties until March 31, 2026), the BVI register became operational for 'legitimate interest' access from April 1, 2026. The register is not public; entities may apply for exemptions preventing disclosure on legitimate interest grounds, though competent authorities and law enforcement retain full access. **What it means for clients:** Clients with BVI companies must ensure their beneficial ownership information is filed with the BVI register; while the register is not publicly searchable, competent authorities and law enforcement have unrestricted access, and third parties with a demonstrable legitimate interest may apply for access.

## Cyprus

### ℹ️ FYI — Cyprus comprehensive tax reform in force from January 1, 2026 — corporate rate 15%, crypto 8%, reduced SDC
**What changed:** Approved December 22, 2025 and effective January 1, 2026, Cyprus's tax reform raises the corporate income tax rate from 12.5% to 15% (aligning with OECD Pillar Two). A flat 8% tax applies to gains from crypto asset disposals and share-based remuneration. The Special Defence Contribution on dividends was reduced from 17% to 5% for actual distributions. Non-domiciled individuals retain 17-year exemptions on worldwide dividend, interest and investment gains; no estate, inheritance, wealth or gift taxes apply.
**What it means for clients:** Cyprus holding structures face a higher corporate tax rate (15%) but the reduced 5% SDC on actual dividend distributions may partially offset the impact; non-domiciled residents retain their extensive exemptions, and the new 8% crypto gains tax requires clients with crypto held through Cyprus entities to reassess their tax position.
**Source:** Sovereign Group — 2026-01-01 — https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/cyprus-brings-comprehensive-tax-reform-into-force/
