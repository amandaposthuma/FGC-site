## BVI

### ⚠️ URGENT — BVI BOI Penalty Moratorium Ended March 31, 2026 – Four-Tier Penalty Regime Now Fully Active
**What changed:** The BVI FSC's moratorium on BOI filing fees and penalties ended March 31, 2026. As of April 1, 2026, the full four-tier penalty regime is active with fines of US$10,000–US$75,000 depending on nature, duration, and impact of the contravention. BVI companies registered after January 2, 2025 must file BOI within 30 days of formation; pre-existing companies with late filings have been accruing penalties since April 1. The legitimate interest access regime also opened April 1, 2026, allowing AML-obligated persons to request UBO information for holdings at or above 25%.
**What it means for clients:** BVI holding companies, SPVs, and fund vehicles that have not filed beneficial ownership information are in active violation with fines potentially reaching US$75,000 per entity. FGC must immediately identify any clients with BVI structures that have not yet submitted BOI and prioritise their remediation to stop penalty accrual.

## Brazil

### 👁 WATCH — Brazil LC 227/2026 – Mandatory Progressive ITCMD Rates Nationwide by January 1, 2027
**What changed:** Lei Complementar 227/2026 mandates that all Brazilian states adopt progressive ITCMD (inheritance and gift tax) rates within the constitutional 8% ceiling by January 1, 2027. The law fills the long-standing constitutional gap on cross-border inheritances and donations and expressly subjects foreign-held assets to Brazilian ITCMD. For São Paulo-sited assets, the effective rate will move from a flat 4% to a progressive scale reaching 8%.
**What it means for clients:** Brazilian HNW clients face an effective doubling of the inheritance and gift tax rate on São Paulo assets from January 1, 2027; cross-border structures involving foreign-held assets are also now expressly in scope. FGC should initiate succession planning reviews immediately — gifting programmes, trust funding, or inter-generational transfers executed before year-end may avoid significantly higher tax cost.
**Source:** Tersi Advogados — 2026-01-01 — https://tersi.adv.br/en/itcmd-in-2026-what-changed-for-gifts-and-inheritances/

### ℹ️ FYI — BCB New Capital Requirement Phase-In Begins July 1, 2026
**What changed:** As of July 1, 2026, Brazilian financial institutions began phasing in new capital thresholds under updated BCB regulations: 25% of the incremental requirement by December 2026, 50% by June 2027, 75% by December 2027, and full compliance by January 2028. The staged transition may modestly affect lending conditions at Brazilian banks.
**What it means for clients:** FGC clients with Brazilian bank-sourced financing — including real estate leverage, company acquisition loans, or structured credit — should monitor whether tightening capital ratios at their Brazilian lenders affect credit availability or cost during the 2026–2028 phase-in period.
**Source:** Global Legal Insights — 2026-07-01 — https://www.globallegalinsights.com/practice-areas/banking-and-finance-laws-and-regulations/brazil/

### ℹ️ FYI — Brazil 10% Dividend Withholding Tax on Large Distributions – Effective January 2026
**What changed:** Law 15.270/2025 (effective January 2026) imposes a 10% withholding tax on dividends exceeding R$50,000 in a single month paid to domestic individuals, and on all dividends paid to non-residents regardless of amount. This reverses Brazil's long-standing dividend exemption.
**What it means for clients:** FGC clients holding Brazilian operating companies, private equity interests, or real-estate funds should ensure their dividend distributions since January 2026 have been restructured to account for the 10% withholding cost, and that non-resident shareholders are receiving net payments with correct withholding applied.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients who are Luxembourg fund managers, investment advisers, or board members with carried interest entitlements should confirm with their Luxembourg tax advisers that their carry documentation and tax treatment aligns with the new regime for fiscal year 2026.
