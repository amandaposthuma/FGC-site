## Brazil

### ⚠️ URGENT — BCB Resolution 561 – Stablecoin Cross-Border Remittance Ban in Force; VASP Authorisation Deadline 30 October 2026
**What changed:** BCB Resolution 561 (effective 1 October 2026) prohibits payment firms from settling cross-border remittances in crypto assets, including dollar-pegged stablecoins. In addition, existing VASPs must apply for BCB authorisation by 30 October 2026; after that date, regulated banks and financial institutions may not service unauthorised crypto companies.
**What it means for clients:** Brazilian-resident clients or clients with Brazilian business operations who use stablecoin rails for cross-border transfers must transition to alternative remittance channels immediately, as such transfers became unlawful on 1 October. Any client exposed to a Brazilian VASP (exchange, crypto payment processor) must verify that the VASP has or has applied for BCB authorisation before 30 October, or risk losing banking services for that counterparty.
**Source:** FCM Law – Brazil VASP Licence: BCB Rules, Deadlines and FAQ (2026) — 2026-10-01 — https://fcm.law/brazil-vasp-license/

### 👁 WATCH — Supplementary Law 227/2026 – ITCMD Now Levied on Overseas Assets at Market Value
**What changed:** Supplementary Law No. 227/2026 (in force January 2026) requires mandatory market-value assessment of inherited or gifted assets in Brazil and extends ITCMD (inheritance and gift tax) jurisdiction to overseas assets where the beneficiary is Brazilian. Tax jurisdiction is determined by the nature and location of assets.
**What it means for clients:** HNW Brazilian clients with overseas trusts, foreign real estate, or offshore investment portfolios now face potential ITCMD exposure on those assets when transferred to Brazilian beneficiaries – a fundamental change that did not previously exist. Existing estate plans built on the assumption that offshore assets were beyond ITCMD scope should be reviewed and likely restructured.
**Source:** Chambers Private Wealth 2026 – Brazil — 2026-01-01 — https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/private-wealth-2026/brazil

### 👁 WATCH — Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act 2026 – Expanded Scope to Private Funds and Trusts
**What changed:** The Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act 2026 expanded disclosure requirements to private funds, mutual funds, and trust structures, lowered reporting thresholds, and introduced a 'legitimate interest' access model (not fully public). **What it means for clients:** Clients with Cayman Islands private funds, mutual funds, or trust structures that were previously outside beneficial ownership registration requirements are now in scope and must file disclosure information with the Cayman registry; failure to comply risks deregistration as CIMA accelerates enforcement ahead of its 2027 FATF evaluation.

## BVI

### 👁 WATCH — BVI Beneficial Ownership Register – Legitimate Interest Access Open; Threshold Lowered to 10%
**What changed:** From 1 April 2026, the BVI Registry accepts legitimate interest applications to access beneficial ownership records. The threshold for disclosure as a beneficial owner was lowered from 25% to 10%, significantly expanding the universe of individuals who must be registered.
**What it means for clients:** Clients with BVI companies where minority shareholders or controllers hold between 10% and 25% must now be registered as beneficial owners – a category that was previously exempt – and those individuals' names and interests may be accessible to parties demonstrating legitimate interest, increasing transparency risk for clients who relied on the prior 25% threshold for privacy.

## Luxembourg

### 👁 WATCH — Luxembourg 2026 Budget – Carried Interest Exemption, SPF Reform, and Start-up Tax Credit
**What changed:** For tax year 2026, Luxembourg introduced a carried interest tax exemption for AIF participations held more than six months representing no more than 10% of fund share capital, authorised SPFs to be established as simplified joint-stock companies, and introduced a 20% income tax credit (capped at €100,000 per investor per year) on investments of at least €10,000 in eligible start-ups.
**What it means for clients:** Fund managers and general partners with Luxembourg AIF structures can now structure eligible carried interest with a tax exemption, potentially reducing effective tax rates on performance fees significantly; HNW investors in Luxembourg SPFs benefit from a more flexible corporate form, while angel/early-stage investors in eligible start-ups can claim meaningful tax credits against Luxembourg income.

## Panama

### 👁 WATCH — Panama Decree 25/2026 – FATCA/CRS Controlling Person Threshold Reduced from 25% to 10%
**What changed:** Panama's Ministry of Economy and Finance reduced the ownership/control threshold for reportable controlling persons under FATCA and CRS from 25% to 10% via Decree No. 25 (June 2026). Reporting institutions may rely on AML/KYC procedures to treat trust settlors and foundation founders as controlling persons. SBP Rule 1-2026 also added enhanced BOI requirements for FinTech entities.
**What it means for clients:** Clients with Panama foundations, trusts, or corporate vehicles where individuals control 10%–25% of the structure now fall into the reportable controlling person category under both FATCA and CRS, meaning their tax residency information will be reported to relevant foreign tax authorities – a significant expansion of automatic tax information exchange exposure for Panama-based wealth structures.

## USA

### ℹ️ FYI — FinCEN Final Rule – BOI Reporting Permanently Eliminated for US Domestic Companies (August 2026)
**What changed:** Effective 14 August 2026, FinCEN permanently removed BOI reporting requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act for all US domestic companies and US persons. Previously filed BOI for US persons will be deleted. Foreign companies registered to do business in the US remain subject to BOI requirements.
**What it means for clients:** US-domiciled clients operating through US domestic entities no longer have any BOI filing obligations and need not maintain or update prior submissions; however, clients who use foreign entities registered in the US (e.g., BVI or Cayman vehicles registered as foreign reporting companies) must continue to meet BOI requirements and should confirm compliance status.

### ℹ️ FYI — Florida SB 264 – Foreign Buyer Real Estate Restrictions Upheld; Affidavit Requirement Ongoing
**What changed:** Florida SB 264, restricting real estate purchases by foreign principals from seven 'countries of concern' (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Syria, Venezuela/Maduro regime), was upheld by courts in November 2025. All buyers must sign compliance affidavits at closing. Chinese-connected buyers face the broadest restrictions on any Florida real property.
**What it means for clients:** Clients with connections to any of the seven designated countries must ensure full SB 264 compliance at every Florida property closing, including accurate affidavit completion; violations carry property forfeiture and felony charges, so any client with potential country-of-concern links should be reviewed prior to executing a Florida real estate transaction.

### ℹ️ FYI — FIRPTA – 15% Withholding Rate Applies to Foreign Sellers of US Real Property in 2026
**What changed:** FIRPTA withholding for foreign sellers of US real property remains at 15% in 2026 (10% for personal residences with a sale price not exceeding $1 million). Final regulations under Section 892 governing foreign government investment were released December 2025. The FIRPTA exemption for domestically controlled REITs is fully operative.
**What it means for clients:** Foreign clients selling US real property must factor the 15% FIRPTA withholding rate into their transaction planning and liquidity, as the withheld amount is applied to the purchase price, not the gain; buyers bear the withholding obligation, and clients with REIT investments should confirm the domestically controlled exemption applies to their specific holding to avoid unnecessary over-withholding.
