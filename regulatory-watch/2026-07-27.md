## BVI

### 👁 WATCH — BVI Legitimate Interest Access to UBO Register Now Active (April 2026)

**What changed:** Since April 1, 2026, persons and institutions demonstrating a 'legitimate interest' connected to AML, counter-terrorist financing, or counter-proliferation financing can access BVI beneficial ownership information — including name, month/year of birth, nationality, and nature of interest — for individuals owning or controlling 25% or more. Clients should review whether their BVI vehicles remain fit for purpose given their personal risk tolerance and profile.

### 👁 WATCH — OECD Pillar Two Side-by-Side Safe Harbor for US Multinationals – Effective 2026

**What changed:** On January 5, 2026, the OECD Inclusive Framework agreed a Side-by-Side Safe Harbor that exempts US-parented multinational groups from the Income Inclusion Rule and Undertaxed Profits Rule, based on the sufficiency of US domestic tax law. Effective for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2026. The US is currently the only jurisdiction listed in the OECD Central Record qualifying for this arrangement.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients who are US citizens or tax residents holding foreign subsidiaries or MNE groups may now qualify for this safe harbor, substantially reducing Pillar Two compliance complexity and cost — but the analysis must be done structure-by-structure, as qualification depends on the specific US tax footprint. Non-US clients holding US-parented groups are similarly affected.

## UAE

### 👁 WATCH — UAE Capital Markets Authority (CMA) Replaces SCA – New Framework from January 2026

## Cyprus

### 👁 WATCH — Cyprus Comprehensive Tax Reform Effective January 1, 2026

**What changed:** Cyprus enacted a major tax reform effective January 1, 2026: corporate income tax raised from 12.5% to 15%; SDC on dividends reduced from 17% to 5% for Cyprus tax-resident and domiciled individuals; crypto asset gains taxed at 8%; loss carry-forward extended from 5 to 7 years; Deemed Dividend Distribution rules abolished. The non-domicile regime remains intact.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients using Cyprus holding companies face a higher CIT rate (now 15%, aligned with Pillar Two) but benefit from a dramatically lower dividend SDC (5% vs 17%) if they are Cyprus-resident and domiciled — the net effect must be modelled per client. Non-domicile clients remain fully exempt from SDC, preserving the regime's core attraction for international HNW clients.

**Source:** Harneys Fiduciary — 2026-01-01 — https://www.harneysfiduciary.com/insights/cyprus-tax-reform-2026-key-changes-for-businesses-and-individuals/

## Bahamas

### 👁 WATCH — Bahamas Enacts Usufruct Interest Act, 2026 – New Wealth Structuring Tool

**What changed:** The Bahamas enacted the Usufruct Interest Act, 2026 on March 23, 2026, establishing the jurisdiction's first comprehensive statutory usufruct framework. The Act enables formal separation of beneficial enjoyment from legal ownership over a broad asset class including equity interests, securities, intellectual property, digital assets, receivables, and real property. Usufruct interests may be publicly registered.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients engaged in succession planning or intergenerational wealth transfer now have access to a formally registered Bahamian usufruct as an alternative to trusts or foundations, particularly useful for separating income rights from capital rights across generations or for cross-border estate structures where forced heirship rules in the client's home jurisdiction might otherwise complicate planning.

## Isle of Man

### 👁 WATCH — Isle of Man Lowers UBO Threshold to 25% (Effective May 25, 2026)

**What changed:** Amendments in force from May 25, 2026 lower the Isle of Man's registrable beneficial ownership threshold from 'more than 25%' to '25% or more', bringing individuals holding exactly 25% into scope. A more detailed, judgment-based approach to control assessments for trusts, foundations, and governance arrangements was also introduced. **What it means for clients:** FGC clients with Isle of Man entities where any individual holds precisely a 25% stake must ensure their beneficial ownership register is updated to include those individuals; clients using IoM trusts or foundations should also expect their fiduciary service providers to apply the new judgment-based control test and may receive requests to clarify governance arrangements.

## USA

### 👁 WATCH — US Federal Estate Tax Exemption Made Permanent at $15M per Person (OBBBA)

**What changed:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, made the TCJA's elevated estate and gift tax exemptions permanent and raised them to $15 million per person ($30 million per married couple) effective January 1, 2026, indexed for inflation from 2027. Non-resident aliens retain only a $60,000 federal estate tax exemption for US-situs assets.

**What it means for clients:** The elimination of the 2026 sunset fundamentally changes the planning calculus for FGC's US-citizen clients — strategies designed to 'use it before you lose it' may now be unnecessary or even sub-optimal and should be reviewed. Conversely, FGC's non-US resident clients holding US situs assets (real estate, US equities) remain exposed to US estate tax with only a $60,000 exemption, a gap that has never been wider relative to the US-citizen exemption and demands active estate planning.

**Source:** Israelo FF Law / Multiple Sources — 2026-01-01 — https://www.israelofflawcpa.com/wills-estate-planning/the-2026-tax-landscape-for-hnwis-why-your-sunset-plans-are-now-permanent/

### 👁 WATCH — FinCEN BOI: Domestic US Companies Remain Exempt; Foreign-Formed Entities Must File

**What changed:** Following FinCEN's March 21, 2025 interim final rule, all US-formed entities and their beneficial owners are exempt from BOI reporting under the CTA. Only foreign-formed companies registered to conduct business in the US must report. A final rule is expected in 2026 and could expand or confirm the current scope.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients with foreign-registered entities (e.g., Cayman, BVI, or Luxembourg companies) that are also registered to do business in a US state must file BOI reports with FinCEN — the exemption applies only to US-formed entities. Clients should audit any foreign entity with US state registrations and confirm compliance.

## USA (Florida)

### ℹ️ FYI — Florida SB 264 – Foreign Buyer Restrictions Remain in Force; Legal Challenges Ongoing

**What changed:** Florida SB 264 (effective July 1, 2023) restricts nationals from designated 'countries of concern' (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela, Syria) from acquiring certain real property in Florida. The law remains in force in 2026. Federal courts have halted enforcement against some individual Chinese plaintiffs pending appeal, but the Eleventh Circuit has otherwise upheld the law. All foreign nationals must complete an affidavit at closing.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients who are nationals of — or have significant ties to — any of the seven designated countries must receive specific legal advice before acquiring Florida property; all other foreign national clients must complete a closing affidavit confirming they are not within the restricted categories. FGC's core Latin American client base is generally not affected by the nationality restrictions but should be advised of the affidavit requirement.

**Source:** Akerman LLP — 2026-01-01 — https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/updates-on-sb-264-florida-law.html

### ℹ️ FYI — FIRPTA Withholding Remains at 15% for Foreign Sellers; Non-Resident Estate Exemption $60K

**What changed:** FIRPTA withholding for foreign persons selling US real property interests remains at 15% of gross sales price for 2026, with no rate changes. Non-resident alien estate tax exemption for US-situs assets is $60,000, versus the $15 million exemption now available to US persons under the OBBBA.

**What it means for clients:** FGC clients who are foreign sellers of US real estate should budget for a 15% FIRPTA withholding at closing (recoverable via tax return if actual gain is lower) and should confirm that their US tax counsel has a withholding certificate strategy in place. The $60,000 non-resident estate exemption compared to the new $15M US-citizen exemption highlights a critical planning gap for international clients holding US situs assets — estate planning documents for these clients should be reviewed.
