## Switzerland

### 👁 WATCH — Switzerland's centralized Transparency Register for beneficial owners enters into force October 1, 2026
**What changed:** The Federal Act on the Transparency of Legal Entities (TJPG) enters into force October 1, 2026, requiring over 500,000 Swiss entities (stock corporations, GmbH, and similar) to report beneficial owners at the 25%+ threshold to the new electronic Transparency Register via EasyGov. Foreign entities with Swiss branches, Swiss real estate, or de facto Swiss administration are in scope. Listed companies are exempt. Initial reporting obligations may crystallize by end-2026 or early 2027.
**What it means for clients:** HNW clients holding Swiss companies, GmbH interests, or Swiss real estate through entities must identify their beneficial owners and prepare for registration on EasyGov from October 1; foreign entities with Swiss nexus are also covered, making this relevant for clients who use Swiss holding or real estate structures. Advisors should identify all in-scope entities in client portfolios now and coordinate with Swiss counsel on the registration timeline and documentation.
**Source:** CMS Law / Bär & Karrer — 2026-08-01 — https://cms.law/en/che/legal-updates/new-obligations-in-switzerland-to-report-to-centralised-electronic-register-of-beneficial-ownership-effective-1-october-2026

## USA

### 👁 WATCH — U.S. Treasury confirms U.S.-headquartered multinationals are exempt from OECD Pillar Two domestic implementation
**What changed:** In January 2026 the U.S. Treasury announced that U.S.-headquartered multinational companies are exempt from Pillar Two requirements domestically and the U.S. will not implement Pillar Two. However, U.S.-parented groups operating in Pillar Two jurisdictions may still face top-up taxes (DMTT or IIR) levied by those foreign countries on U.S.-source income, creating an asymmetric exposure.
**What it means for clients:** HNW clients who own U.S.-parented multinational businesses with revenues over €750 million and operations in EU member states, UAE, Switzerland, or other Pillar Two jurisdictions face potential foreign top-up tax charges on their U.S. entities — advisors should map each client group's jurisdictional footprint against DMTT-implementing countries and model any incremental tax cost. U.S. clients with purely domestic operations are unaffected.
**Source:** Moody's Analytics / OECD — 2026-01-15 — https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/insights/public-sector/understanding-pillar-two-the-global-minimum-tax-policy.html

### ℹ️ FYI — FinCEN final rule permanently exempts U.S. companies from BOI reporting under Corporate Transparency Act
**What changed:** FinCEN issued a final rule effective August 14, 2026 permanently eliminating BOI reporting requirements for U.S.-formed companies and U.S. persons under the CTA. Foreign companies registered in the U.S. remain subject to BOI reporting but need not report their U.S. person beneficial owners. FinCEN is deleting previously reported information from now-exempt U.S. persons.
**What it means for clients:** U.S.-formed entities no longer have any BOI reporting obligation to FinCEN — clients who had prepared or filed BOI reports for U.S. LLCs, corporations, or partnerships can discontinue that compliance track. Foreign companies with U.S. registrations must still file BOI reports but can exclude U.S. citizen or resident beneficial owners from those reports.
**Source:** Federal Register / U.S. Treasury FinCEN — 2026-08-14 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision

### ℹ️ FYI — Federal estate and gift tax exemption permanently raised to $15 million per person effective January 1, 2026
**What changed:** The 'One Big Beautiful Bill Act' (signed July 4, 2025) permanently eliminated the prior-law sunset and set the federal estate and gift tax exemption at $15 million per individual ($30 million per couple), indexed for inflation from 2027. The 40% rate continues above the exemption. Annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per donee ($38,000 for married couples).
**What it means for clients:** U.S.-connected HNW clients now have significantly more room for lifetime gifting and estate planning under the permanent $15 million exemption — advisors should review whether existing irrevocable trusts and gifting programs fully utilize the expanded capacity, particularly for clients who had previously held back gifting in anticipation of a sunset. Portability elections remain important for surviving spouses.
**Source:** Seyfarth Shaw LLP — 2026-01-01 — https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/planning-for-2026-trusts-and-estates-tax-updates.html

## USA (Florida)

### 👁 WATCH — Florida SB 264 foreign buyer restrictions confirmed enforceable by 11th Circuit; FIRPTA withholding remains at 15%
**What changed:** The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit (Shen v. Simpson, November 4, 2025) upheld Florida SB 264: Chinese nationals face near-total prohibition on any Florida real estate purchase; nationals of Russia, Cuba, Venezuela, Iran, Syria, and North Korea are prohibited from purchasing within 10 miles of military or critical infrastructure sites. Buyer affidavits are required; violations carry forfeiture risk and criminal penalties. FIRPTA withholding remains at 15% of gross sales price for all foreign sellers.
**What it means for clients:** FGC clients who are nationals of the seven restricted countries must be advised that they cannot purchase Florida real estate (Chinese nationals) or must verify distances from restricted zones before closing (all others); buyers must sign affidavits at closing and risk criminal prosecution for violations. Existing foreign owners selling Florida property remain subject to 15% FIRPTA withholding, which must be factored into net proceeds calculations.
**Source:** South Florida Law PLLC — 2026-01-18 — https://www.southfloridalawpllc.com/2026/01/18/what-restrictions-do-foreign-real-estate-buyers-face-in-florida/
